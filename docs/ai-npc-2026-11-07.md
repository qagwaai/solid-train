# Project Stellar: Local AI NPC Integration and Narrative Architecture

Date: 2026-11-07

Status: **Planning only.** This document captures the initial prompt and the
user's answers to its interactive setup form, including current planning
constraints and preferences. It does not define a model, runtime protocol,
database schema, or authorize integration work.

## 1. Executive Summary and AI Narrative Vision

The goal is to explore local AI-driven NPCs that add conversational variability
and constrained personalities to *Project Stellar*, while preserving authored
narrative intent. The starting game experience must work offline and without
AI inference; local AI responses are an optional addition.
The scope should consider both casual day-to-day conversations and support for
longer story arcs spanning multiple acts.

Potential character and gameplay contexts include:

- Early-game mentors and survival companions, including examples such as Jax or
  Elara, who could accompany the player through early survival and scavenging.
- Station brokers and faction contacts for trade-related conversations and
  localized rumors.
- Late-game neural-link or fleet operators who reflect the player's progression
  toward broader, potentially remote influence.
- Antagonists, including small-time thieves and larger crime bosses. They may
  use patterns similar to the market manager or companion while pursuing goals
  that differ in their relationship to the player character.
- Event-driven AI scenarios are a core capability to support in the minimum
  AI-enabled tier; specific event sources and workload remain undefined. The
  baseline game must remain playable without AI.

These roles are part of the current planning scope, though not a commitment to
implement every role or character.
The desired narrative progression—from a fragile early experience toward a
broader late-game presence—is a useful framing question for the next design
session, not an approved story structure.

For the next planning work, the user wants to focus on two contrasting cases:

- **Market manager:** A trade-oriented NPC who buys and sells raw materials.
  Their dialog should vary greetings across visits; repeating a greeting
  sometimes is acceptable. With AI enabled, this NPC should explain market and
  material availability, discuss or negotiate trade terms, and share rumors or
  market news. It may propose buy/sell prices and quantities for the game to
  use subject to game-rule validation. Without AI, a relatively simple market
  demand algorithm should provide prices/quantities, with trade guidance and
  valid buying/selling still available.
- **Companion:** A character whose scope may include emotional growth,
  relationship-building, and a broader set of in-depth dialogue options.
  Emotional growth should be visible gradually based on character actions.
  Potential influences include conversation choices and treatment of the
  companion, major story milestones, actions outside dialogue, time and repeated
  interactions, and the player-character context.

These are planning examples, not implementation commitments. They likely have
different requirements: the market manager centers on repeated interactions
around trade, while the companion may need more extensive relationship and
narrative continuity. Shared foundations may still apply; the distinction
should be tested as requirements are developed. The exact relationship rules,
timing, and visible signals remain undecided.
Antagonists are also in the current planning scope; small-time thieves and
larger crime bosses may reuse these conversational patterns while having
different goals related to the player character. They can use the same
high-level memory and event-awareness concepts, with antagonist-specific
motives. Crime bosses in particular may rely heavily on private information.
AI may propose market prices and quantities, but the game validates outcomes
before applying authoritative market state and trade execution. With AI
unavailable, a relatively simple market demand algorithm supplies
prices/quantities instead.

## 2. Local Tech Stack and Runtime Architecture

The intended direction is to investigate a local, Node.js-backed NPC capability.
The initial prompt proposed a local bridge for assembling prompts, injecting
game-state context, and maintaining a rolling conversation window. The user
confirmed that this bridge is an area of interest.

The game must run without an AI model or local runner. If the player wants AI
NPC responses, the game should be able to connect to an external local runner
such as LM Studio. The runner is user-installed and launched separately; the
game documentation should explain how to configure the connection. Bundling a
model/runtime is not supported in the current planning scope. The specific
runner, model, connection protocol, and failure handling are not selected.
LM Studio is an initial example to evaluate, while alternative local runners
remain open for comparison.
If the runner is unavailable, the player should be able to retry or configure
it, or continue without AI.

The starting experience should work offline. Optional online inference may be
considered later if a player chooses to pay for tokens; this is a possible
future option, not part of the initial scope or an approved service design.

Runner integration should consider connection setup, offline operation without
internet or cloud services, control over runtime/model versions and updates,
isolation from the game process, and compatibility with GPU resources shared
with Nova.

Response performance remains unbenchmarked. The provisional expectation is to
show the first visible token within less than five seconds. There is no
completion-time target until benchmarking. There is no response-length or token
limit yet.
Natural-sounding dialogue is desirable for some NPCs, while others may need
greater narrative depth. The market manager should have concise greetings and
routine trade exchanges; companion dialogue may be deeper and
context-dependent. Numeric response-length limits should eventually differ by
NPC type, but no values or measurement units are set.

Hardware requirements should distinguish the baseline game (playable without
AI) from AI-enabled NPC functionality. At least one limited NPC interaction
should have a CPU-only fallback. A market-manager greeting/basic trade
interaction or a companion greeting/everyday exchange may serve, depending on
feasibility; the specific interaction and its performance and quality bar are
not yet specified. A relatively strong GPU is expected to be available for NPC
models, but Nova retains priority and NPC inference should yield or be limited
as needed. The enforcement mechanism and resource limits are undecided. The
exact GPU model, CPU, RAM, VRAM, disk, model size, latency, and quality
requirements remain undecided. Establish the AI-enabled minimum by benchmarking
the required workloads together, including event-driven scenarios and Nova's
GPU use, rather than choosing a GPU model up front. The baseline game and
AI-enabled requirements remain separate tiers.

The project is not targeting multiplayer at this time. The expected player
environment should have CPU, RAM, and other resources available to run the
backend locally, but the actual minimum remains an open question.

## 3. Personality Constraints and Game-State Context

The NPC experience should offer conversational variability without losing
character identity or narrative constraints. Future design should consider how
characters remain within their intended personalities, knowledge, and roles,
and how conversations respond to relevant game state. No prompt format,
personality representation, macro syntax, or state-mapping contract has been
selected.

The initial prompt's example variables—such as
`{{player_fleet_tier}}` and `{{npc_relationship_score}}`—are illustrative only.
Potential context includes player progression, fleet status, relationship
changes, faction alignment, and current events, but the authoritative sources
and permitted visibility of those values are undecided. Candidate knowledge
sources include authored/seeded character history, current system-specific
facts, noteworthy events or news, player progression, actions and relationships,
and market/trade state. This is enough to guide planning, but is not a complete
or finalized list. NPC knowledge may vary by role, history, and relationship.
Private-information areas relevant to crime bosses may include market
transactions/supply/pricing, faction or organization operations, and system-wide
security or infrastructure events. Character-specific secrets may come into
play after an event brings an NPC and player character into contact; exact
acquisition rules remain open. The player may manage multiple characters, and
cross-character player knowledge could affect NPC interactions, but its scope
and sharing rules are undefined.
The specific rules for what each NPC knows, when knowledge changes, and what
remains unknown are still undefined. Knowledge of events should propagate at
least at light speed, with ships able to carry information faster and jump gates
enabling especially rapid spread. Information reaches NPCs as a spreading wave
with some jumps; an NPC does not know an event until its knowledge has reached
them. Reports may be incomplete, delayed, or distorted; rumors are possible.
Event information may be public, private/restricted, faction- or
organization-specific, or secret. Knowledge depends on propagation and
visibility. Visibility can change, for example when a secret leaks or is
officially released; the rules for those changes remain open.

Both routine interactions (for example, repeated chats with a broker) and
longer-term story development should be considered. Event-triggered dialogue or
AI scenarios are a core capability to support in the minimum AI-enabled tier.
The baseline game must remain playable without AI. Scenarios should be
triggered by game events; NPCs/AI respond to those events rather than
initiating them. Potential trigger examples
include player actions or milestones, system-wide newsworthy events, market
changes, relationship/story state changes, and scheduled or ambient events, but
no particular trigger source is yet a minimum requirement. The event sources,
workload, compute budget, and eventing system are deferred for later definition.

## 4. Story Arc Memory and Persistence Layer

The user wants to explore both short-term conversational context and
longer-term story-arc support. A persistent relationship or story ledger is
within scope, including the initial prompt's examples of milestones, hostility
flags, and emotional shifts. These examples do not define an approved schema or
set of persisted state.

State should persist across backend restarts and game sessions in a database.
When the backend starts, the current player/character relationships with NPCs
will need to be seeded into its state. NPC memory should be scoped per
character. Potential player knowledge shared across multiple characters is a
separate unresolved question from per-character NPC memory. The game currently
has no save slots; data is updated continually,
so how memory should behave if save/revert functionality is introduced later
is deferred. Candidate NPC-memory data includes companion relationship and
emotional development, story milestones and consequential events, conversation
summaries or selected memories, and per-NPC knowledge updates. Market/trade
state remains owned by game systems and may provide NPC context. The user
would prefer not to discard conversation data, while recognizing that retaining
full transcripts can grow the database. A hybrid approach—full transcripts for
recent conversations and summaries for older ones—is an option to evaluate.
Whether older raw transcripts are kept in an archive or replaced by summaries
is undecided. The preference is to preserve data if feasible, but storage
impact should be evaluated before setting a retention policy. "Fuzzy memory"
means recall should be intentionally imperfect and vary by NPC and relationship;
what an NPC recalls is separate from what is retained.

Use the existing MongoDB for persistence rather than introducing SQLite or
JSON-backed storage for this feature. The data model, collection ownership,
seed/update lifecycle, and authored narrative milestone representation remain
undecided. Rolling chat context and persistent narrative state should be
treated as separate concepts during future design rather than assuming an
implementation for either.

Game systems should own authoritative game state; the NPC memory layer should
store narrative recall rather than act as a source of truth for game outcomes.
Market/trade state remains owned by game systems. Relationship changes and
other state-changing outcomes are subject to game-rule validation, while NPC
memory may record their narrative significance.

For initial planning, reset behavior can remain simple: remove the relevant
persisted NPC data and restart the backend. The actual reset operation, data
ownership, seed/update lifecycle, future save/revert relationship, and a
production-safe reset experience remain to be designed.

## 5. Guardrails and Hybrid Fallbacks

The planning work should examine how to preserve reliable game progression and
authored story outcomes when dialogue is generated. The initial prompt raised
deterministic quest gates and canned terminal responses as possible
complements to generative dialogue. They are candidate topics, not a selected
hybrid architecture.

The high-level guardrail is that AI may propose actions, but game rules validate
outcomes. Quest gates and progression, market prices/inventory/trade execution,
player/NPC relationship changes, and NPC event triggers/consequences should
remain governed by authoritative game rules. The AI may propose buy/sell prices
and quantities for the game to validate and use. With AI unavailable, a
relatively simple market demand algorithm supplies prices and quantities.
Generated dialogue may vary, but does not itself determine state changes. The
validation and action interfaces are not designed yet.

Fallback behavior is a specific open requirement. The user expects a fallback
and wants the complete game to remain playable without AI inference. In that
mode, all quest-critical interactions and progression must remain available;
players must be able to access markets and valid raw-material buying/selling,
and use essential companion interactions needed to continue the game. Every NPC
should have a complete authored conversation option. Optional generative/social
conversations may be omitted. Possible supporting approaches include authored
deterministic dialogue, predefined conversation trees, and a required CPU-only
path for at least one limited interaction. The exact authored content and
implementation are not designed.
